# Data subject request procedure

A **data subject request** (DSR) is when a person asks the school about personal data it holds about them (or about their child). Under Act 843 people can ask to **see** their data, have it **corrected**, have it **deleted** when it is no longer needed, or **object** to a use of it.

**Policy deadline: answer within 21 days of receiving the request.** The system sets the due date automatically. **[CONFIRM]** the statutory response period and any fee rules with the Data Protection Commission; 21 days is the school's policy until confirmed.

## Who does what

| Step | Who | In the system |
|---|---|---|
| Receive and log the request (in person, letter, email, phone) | Front office (REGISTRAR) or anyone with access | **Data protection → Requests → New**: requester, relationship, student or guardian, type, date received |
| Check identity | Data protection lead | Ask for a Ghana Card or compare with the guardian record. For a child's data, the requester must be a linked guardian with parental responsibility **[CONFIRM]** custody cases with a lawyer |
| Handle | Data protection lead (ADMIN) | Status → *in progress* |
| Respond and close | Data protection lead | Status → *completed* or *rejected*, with the outcome written in plain words |

## By request type

### Access ("Show me what you hold")

1. In the student's record: **Data protection → Subject access export**. The system gathers profile, guardians, enrolments, attendance, marks, report cards, health record, sick-bay visits, incidents, documents list, finance and the list of staff who opened the record. Every export is audited.
2. Read the pack before handing it over. **Remove information about other people** (for example another child named in an incident description, or a staff member's private note) unless they agree **[CONFIRM]** the exemptions with a lawyer (for example where disclosure could harm the child).
3. Give it to the verified requester (printed or on a USB stick handed over in person; never by WhatsApp).
4. Record the date and what was given in the request outcome.

### Correction ("This is wrong")

1. Check the evidence (for example a birth certificate for a date of birth).
2. Correct the record in the normal screen (the change is audited with before/after values).
3. Close the request with a note of what changed. If the school disagrees, explain why in writing and record the parent's view in the outcome.

### Deletion ("Delete my data")

1. Check whether the school still needs the data: a current pupil's records are needed to educate the child; finance records must be kept for the statutory period.
2. If the child has left and nothing requires keeping the record, **anonymise** the student (Data protection → Anonymise; type the student code to confirm). This is irreversible.
3. If the school must keep some data, explain which and why, and delete or anonymise the rest.

### Objection ("Stop using my data for …")

Examples: stop SMS announcements (the guardian's "receives SMS" switch), stop using a photo. Act on it unless the school has a strong reason it must continue (for example a safety message in an emergency). Record the decision.

## Overdue requests

The request list shows **Overdue** in red when a request passes its due date and is not closed. The data protection lead checks the list every week.

## Complaints

If the requester is unhappy, tell them they may complain to the **Data Protection Commission**, and give the DPC's current contact details (keep them up to date in this document): _[DPC address, phone, website]_.
